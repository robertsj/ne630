Problem 2 [FNRP 1.5] Show problem

The average kinetic energy of a fission neutron is 2.0 MeV. Defining the kinetic energy as $K = E_{\text{total}} - m_0 c^2$, what is the percent error introduced into the kinetic energy from using Eq. (1.12) instead of Eq. (1.9)? (Note that $v$ should be computed using relativistic expressions and that the error should be defined relative to the true value, i.e., $\%\text{err} = 100(K_{\text{approximate}}-K_{\text{true}})/K_{\text{true}}$, where the “true” value comes from Eq. 1.9 and the “approximate” value comes from Eq. 1.12.)

Problem 3 [FNRP 1.6] Show problem

Consider the following nuclear and chemical reactions:

  • (a) A uranium-235 nucleus fissions as a result of being bombarded by a slow neutron. If the energy of fission is 200 MeV, approximately what fraction of the reactants mass is converted to energy?
  • (b) A carbon-12 atom undergoes combustion following collision with an oxygen-16 molecule ($\mathrm{O}_2$), forming carbon dioxide. If four eV of energy are released, approximately what fraction of the reactants mass is converted to energy?
  • (c) Are the numbers in (a) and (b) consistent with the estimated 20 tons of nuclear fuel per year or the 10,000 tons of coal per day needed for a 1000 MWe nuclear or coal power plant? State your assumptions! (Hint: remember from NE 495 that the number of atoms $N$ in a given mass $m$ is given by $N=mN_a/M$, where $N_a=6.022\cdot 10^{23}~\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$ is Avogadro's number and $M$ is the atomic mass that we often approximate as the mass number $A=Z+N$ in units of grams per mole. Example: in 100 g of ${}^{235}\mathrm{U}$, there are approximately $100(6.022\cdot 10^{23})/235 \approx 2.56\cdot 10^{23}$ atoms.)

Problem 3 [Lewis Eqs. (1.21)--(1.23)] Show problem

An idealized prompt-neutron chain begins with $n_0$ neutrons. Each neutron generation takes an average time $\ell$, and the multiplication factor $k$ is constant.

  • (a) Beginning with $n_{i+1}=kn_i$, derive

    $$ n(t)=n_0 k^{t/\ell}. $$

  • (b) For $k\approx 1$, show that

    $$ n(t)\approx n_0\exp\left(\frac{k-1}{\ell}t\right). $$

  • (c) Classify systems having $k=0.995$, $k=1.000$, and $k=1.005$ as subcritical, critical, or supercritical, and describe the long-term behavior of the neutron population in each system.

  • (d) A reactor initially produces $1.25~\mathrm{MW(t)}$, with $k=1.010$ and $\ell=10^{-5}~\mathrm{s}$. Assuming $P(t)\propto n(t)$, estimate the time required to reach $2.00~\mathrm{MW(t)}$ using both the exact and approximate expressions.

Problem 6 Show problem

Our TRIGA fuel consists of a mixture of zirconium hydride ($\mathrm{ZrH}_{1.6}$) and uranium metal with a bulk mass density of $7.1~\mathrm{g\,cm^{-3}}$.

  • Uranium makes up 8.5% of the mixture by mass.
  • The uranium is enriched to 20% ${}^{235}\mathrm{U}$ by atom; assume the balance is ${}^{238}\mathrm{U}$.
  • Natural zirconium consists of ${}^{90}\mathrm{Zr}$ (51.5%), ${}^{91}\mathrm{Zr}$ (11.2%), ${}^{92}\mathrm{Zr}$ (17.1%), ${}^{94}\mathrm{Zr}$ (17.4%), and ${}^{96}\mathrm{Zr}$ (2.8%), all by atom.
  • Assume that ${}^{1}\mathrm{H}$, ${}^{235}\mathrm{U}$, and ${}^{238}\mathrm{U}$ are the only other nuclides.

Use $N_A=6.022\times10^{23}~\mathrm{mol^{-1}}$ and approximate each isotope's molar mass by its mass number in $\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$.

  • (a) Compute the indicated molecular and atomic number densities. The following expressions provide a starting point:

    $$ \begin{gathered} \overline{M}_{\mathrm{Zr}} =0.515(90)+0.112(91)+0.171(92)+0.174(94)+0.028(96),\\ M_{\mathrm{ZrH}_{1.6}} =\overline{M}_{\mathrm{Zr}}+1.6(1),\\ n_{\mathrm{ZrH}_{1.6}} =\frac{(0.915)(7.1)N_A}{M_{\mathrm{ZrH}_{1.6}}},\\ n_{{}^{1}\mathrm{H}} =1.6n_{\mathrm{ZrH}_{1.6}},\\ n_{{}^{90}\mathrm{Zr}} =0.515n_{\mathrm{ZrH}_{1.6}},\\[3pt] \overline{M}_{\mathrm{U}} =0.20(235)+0.80(238),\\ n_{\mathrm{U}} =\frac{(0.085)(7.1)N_A}{\overline{M}_{\mathrm{U}}},\\ n_{{}^{235}\mathrm{U}} =0.20n_{\mathrm{U}},\\ n_{{}^{238}\mathrm{U}} =0.80n_{\mathrm{U}}. \end{gathered} $$

    Report all six requested densities: $n_{\mathrm{ZrH}_{1.6}}$, $n_{{}^{1}\mathrm{H}}$, $n_{{}^{90}\mathrm{Zr}}$, $n_{\mathrm{U}}$, $n_{{}^{235}\mathrm{U}}$, and $n_{{}^{238}\mathrm{U}}$.

  • (b) Suppose the elastic-scattering cross section for zirconium, averaged over its naturally occurring isotopes, is $6~\mathrm{b}$ at all neutron energies and that all other interactions with zirconium are negligible. Using the cross sections collected above, compute the macroscopic total, elastic-scattering, and absorption cross sections of the fuel at $1~\mathrm{eV}$.

  • (c) If a $1~\mathrm{eV}$ neutron interacts in the fuel, what is the probability that the interaction is an elastic collision?

  • (d) If a $1~\mathrm{eV}$ neutron has an elastic collision, what is the probability that it collides with ${}^{1}\mathrm{H}$?

  • (e) On average, how far does a $1~\mathrm{eV}$ neutron travel in this fuel before undergoing any interaction?

  • (f) If a $1~\mathrm{eV}$ neutron is absorbed, what is the probability that it induces fission?

  • (g) If a $1~\mathrm{eV}$ neutron induces fission, what is the expected number of emitted neutrons? Account for both uranium isotopes by using a fission-rate-weighted value of $\bar{\nu}$.
