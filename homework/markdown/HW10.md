## Problem 1

Use the narrow-resonance (NR) approximation to compare the epithermal flux spectra of homogeneous mixtures of ${}^{1}\mathrm{H}$ and ${}^{238}\mathrm{U}$. Consider hydrogen-to-uranium atom ratios

$$
R=\frac{N_{{}^{1}\mathrm{H}}}{N_{{}^{238}\mathrm{U}}}
=1000,\ 100,\ \text{and }10.
$$

Use OpenMC's Python nuclear-data interface to obtain the ${}^{1}\mathrm{H}$ and ${}^{238}\mathrm{U}$ total microscopic cross sections (ENDF MT 1) at $294~\mathrm{K}$. Follow the `openmc.data.DataLibrary` and `openmc.data.IncidentNeutron` workflow used in the Lesson 10 notebook. No transport calculation is required for this problem.

For a mixture with atom ratio $R$, the total macroscopic cross section is proportional to

$$
\Sigma_{t,R}(E)\propto
R\,\sigma_t^{{}^{1}\mathrm{H}}(E)
+\sigma_t^{{}^{238}\mathrm{U}}(E).
$$

The NR spectrum has the shape

$$
\widetilde{\phi}_R(E)
=\frac{1}{\Sigma_{t,R}(E)E}.
$$

The omitted NR scattering-source factor is constant with energy for a given mixture and therefore cancels when the spectrum is normalized. For each value of $R$, use

$$
\phi_R(E)=
\frac{\widetilde{\phi}_R(E)}
{\widetilde{\phi}_R(1~\mathrm{eV})},
$$

so that $\phi_R(1~\mathrm{eV})=1$.

- **(a)** Plot the three normalized spectra over $1\le E\le100~\mathrm{eV}$. Use a linear energy axis and a logarithmic flux axis.
- **(b)** In a second panel or figure, plot $E\phi_R(E)$ over the same interval so that departures from the undisturbed $1/E$ spectrum are easy to see.
- **(c)** Explain how the moderator-to-fuel ratio affects the depth of the resonance flux depressions and the strength of energy self-shielding.
- **(d)** Without performing an additional numerical integration, rank the three mixtures by their expected ${}^{238}\mathrm{U}$ capture rate per ${}^{238}\mathrm{U}$ nucleus,

  $$
  \frac{R_{\gamma,R}}{N_{{}^{238}\mathrm{U}}}
  =\int_{1~\mathrm{eV}}^{100~\mathrm{eV}}
  \sigma_\gamma^{{}^{238}\mathrm{U}}(E)\phi_R(E)\,dE,
  $$

  when all three spectra have the normalization given above. Explain your ranking using the flux depressions near the capture resonances. Neglect absorption in hydrogen for this comparison.

## Problem 2

*Adapted from FNRP 3.6.*

Consider an infinite, homogeneous mixture containing a moderator $m$ and a resonant fuel $f$. In the epithermal range, the slowing-down balance is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Sigma_t(E)\phi(E)={}&
\int_E^{E/\alpha_f}
\frac{\Sigma_s^f(E')\phi(E')}{(1-\alpha_f)E'}\,dE'\\
&+\int_E^{E/\alpha_m}
\frac{\Sigma_s^m(E')\phi(E')}{(1-\alpha_m)E'}\,dE'\\
={}&I_f(E)+I_m(E).
\end{aligned}
$$

In the wide-resonance (WR) approximation, also called the narrow-resonance infinite-mass approximation, take $A_f\to\infty$ so that $\alpha_f\to1$. Retain the NR assumptions for the moderator contribution: the moderator scattering cross section is smooth, and the surrounding flux has the background shape $C/E$.

- **(a)** Evaluate $I_m(E)$ under the moderator NR assumptions.
- **(b)** Show that

  $$
  \lim_{\alpha_f\to1} I_f(E)
  =\Sigma_s^f(E)\phi(E).
  $$

  You may interpret the shrinking integration interval as a one-rectangle Riemann sum or evaluate the limit algebraically.
- **(c)** Substitute the two scattering-in contributions into the balance equation and solve for $\phi_{\mathrm{WR}}(E)$.
- **(d)** Compare your WR spectrum with the NR result

  $$
  \phi_{\mathrm{NR}}(E)
  \approx
  \frac{C\left(\Sigma_{s,0}^m+\Sigma_{s,0}^f\right)}
  {\Sigma_t(E)E}.
  $$

  Normalize both spectra to the same off-resonance $C/E$ background. Which approximation predicts the deeper resonance flux depression and therefore more energy self-shielding?
- **(e)** Explain the comparison physically. In particular, describe why elastic scattering from an infinite-mass fuel nucleus does not provide a broad scattering-in source across the resonance.
