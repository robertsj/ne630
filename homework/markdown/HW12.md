## Problem 1

Equations (3.50)–(3.53) in FNRP describe how to compute the resonance integral. Use these equations with nuclear data from OpenMC to compute the absorption resonance integral, $I_a$, for ${}^{238}\mathrm{U}$ over $1\le E\le10^5~\mathrm{eV}$.

- **(a)** Compute $I_a$.
- **(b)** How does this value compare with the value in FNRP Table 3.2?
- **(c)** What is the corresponding effective microscopic absorption cross section, $\bar{\sigma}_a$, for a $1/E$ flux spectrum over the same energy interval?

Use absorption, including radiative capture and fission, rather than radiative capture alone. Report the resonance integral and effective cross section in barns.

## Problem 2

A major approximation in Eqs. (3.50)–(3.53) is the explicit assumption that $\phi(E)=1/E$. Instead, use the same nuclear data as in Problem 1, supplemented by the total cross sections needed for the mixture, with the narrow-resonance (NR) spectrum

$$
\phi_{\mathrm{NR}}(E)\propto\frac{1}{\Sigma_t(E)E}.
$$

Consider an infinite, homogeneous 100-to-1 atom mixture of ${}^{1}\mathrm{H}$ and ${}^{238}\mathrm{U}$, where $\Sigma_t(E)$ is the total macroscopic cross section of the mixture.

- **(a)** Compute the effective microscopic absorption cross section, $\bar{\sigma}_a$, for ${}^{238}\mathrm{U}$ over the same interval, $1\le E\le10^5~\mathrm{eV}$, using the NR spectrum as the flux weighting.
- **(b)** Explain why this value differs from the value computed in Problem 1.
