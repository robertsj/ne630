## Problem 1

Consider the hot, full-power conditions from Homework 20, Problem 2(e). In a
PWR, excess reactivity is usually balanced with boric acid dissolved in the
coolant rather than by partially inserting control rods, which would produce
asymmetric axial power distributions. Set $x=0$ and assume

$$
\Sigma^{\mathrm{M}}_{a,2}
=N_{\mathrm{H}}\sigma^{\mathrm{H}}_a
+N_{\mathrm{O}}\sigma^{\mathrm{O}}_a
+N_{\mathrm{B}}\sigma^{\mathrm{B}}_a.
$$

- **(a)** What concentration of ${}^{10}\mathrm{B}$ in the water, in parts per
  million (ppm), is needed to achieve criticality? Use the cross sections in
  FNRP Table 3.2.
- **(b)** Compute the moderator temperature coefficient, $\alpha_m$, with and
  without this boron concentration. Assume that water has a coefficient of
  thermal expansion $\beta_m=0.004~\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. How does adding boric acid
  affect the moderator temperature coefficient?

## Problem 2 [Adapted from FNRP 9.1]

FNRP Table 4.2 gives the four factors for a pressurized water reactor at
$300~\mathrm{K}$. Assume the reactor is fueled with $\mathrm{UO}_2$ pins having diameter
$1.1~\mathrm{cm}$ and density $11.0~\mathrm{g\,cm^{-3}}$. Determine the fuel
temperature coefficient and plot its value from 300 to $1000~\mathrm{K}$.

## Problem 3 [Bonus]

Using the OpenMC model of a PWR fuel element at nominal full-power conditions
and assuming $P_{\mathrm{NL}}=0.95$:

- **(a)** Determine the critical boron concentration in ppm. Recall that the
  model uses `openmc.model.borated_water` to define `water`.
- **(b)** Compute $\alpha_m$ with and without boron and include the uncertainty.
  `openmc.model.borated_water` automatically computes the water density for a
  specified temperature. How do these values compare with the results from
  Problem 1?
- **(c)** Compute $\alpha_f$ with and without boron and include the uncertainty.
  Set the fuel temperature with `uo2.temperature = T_fuel`; broadened-resonance
  data are available at 600, 900, and $1200~\mathrm{K}$. How does the result
  compare with Problem 2 at the same fuel temperature?

For independent quantities $x$ and $y$ with uncertainties $\sigma_x$ and
$\sigma_y$, the uncertainty of $z=x-y$ is
$\sigma_z=\sqrt{\sigma_x^2+\sigma_y^2}$.
