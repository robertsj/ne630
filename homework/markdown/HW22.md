## Problem 1

This problem builds on Homework 20, Problem 2 and Homework 21, Problem 1.
After several days at full power, the concentration of ${}^{135}\mathrm{Xe}$
in the PWR reaches equilibrium. Assume that the thermal flux in the fuel at
full power is

$$
\phi^{\mathrm{F}}_2=3\times10^{13}~\mathrm{cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}.
$$

Use the iodine–xenon model from Lesson 22, with fission yields and half-lives
from FNRP Chapter 10 and the absorption cross section from FNRP Table 3.2.
For the reactivity comparisons below, use the control-rod model and full-power
conditions from Homework 20, Problem 2(e), changing only the xenon
concentration unless a different control-rod position is specified.

- **(a)** Determine the equilibrium number density of ${}^{135}\mathrm{Xe}$
  in $\mathrm{cm^{-3}}$.
- **(b)** Compute the corresponding macroscopic absorption cross section,
  $\Sigma_a^{\mathrm{fp}}$, in $\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$, using the microscopic cross
  section in FNRP Table 3.2. Here, the superscript $\mathrm{fp}$ denotes the
  fission-product contribution from xenon.
- **(c)** With the control-rod fraction held at $x=x_{\mathrm{fp}}$, the
  full-power critical setting from Homework 20, Problem 2(e), recompute the
  reactivity, $\rho$, including equilibrium ${}^{135}\mathrm{Xe}$. This
  reactivity is called the **equilibrium xenon worth**.
- **(d)** Suppose the reactor is shut down after reaching equilibrium at full
  power. Xenon is no longer removed by neutron absorption, so its
  concentration initially increases, peaks at $11.3~\mathrm{h}$ after
  shutdown, and then decreases as it decays. Compute this peak concentration
  and repeat part (c) to determine the **peak xenon worth**. Does the reactor
  have sufficient excess reactivity to return to full power? Check by
  evaluating the reactivity with the peak xenon concentration and the control
  rods fully withdrawn ($x=0$).

The ability to return to full power after a short shutdown or a period of
low-power operation is particularly important for naval reactors.
