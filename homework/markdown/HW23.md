## Problem 1 [FNRP 10.13]

Consider uranium fuel in a thermal reactor with an initial enrichment of 4%.
For this problem, interpret the enrichment as the atom fraction of
${}^{235}\mathrm{U}$ in the initial uranium, with the balance consisting of
${}^{238}\mathrm{U}$. Assume that no ${}^{239}\mathrm{Pu}$ is initially present.
Use the thermal cross sections in FNRP Table 3.2 and the approximations in
FNRP Eqs. (10.31) and (10.37), including a constant ${}^{238}\mathrm{U}$ number
density.

- **(a)** What is the **conversion ratio (CR)** at the beginning of core life?
- **(b)** What is the conversion ratio after half of the initial
  ${}^{235}\mathrm{U}$ has been consumed; that is, when its number density is
  one-half of its initial value?
- **(c)** At the same point in core life as in part (b), what fraction of the
  total fission power is produced by ${}^{239}\mathrm{Pu}$? Assume equal
  recoverable energy per fission for ${}^{235}\mathrm{U}$ and
  ${}^{239}\mathrm{Pu}$, and neglect fission in ${}^{238}\mathrm{U}$.

**Hint:** Use fluence to identify the point at which half of the initial
${}^{235}\mathrm{U}$ remains, then evaluate the corresponding
${}^{239}\mathrm{Pu}$ inventory.

## Problem 2 [FNRP 10.14]

Thorium-232 is a fertile nuclide that can be transmuted to fissile uranium-233
through the following chain:

$$
{}^{232}_{90}\mathrm{Th}
\xrightarrow{(n,\gamma)}{}^{233}_{90}\mathrm{Th}
\xrightarrow[22~\mathrm{min}]{\beta^-}{}^{233}_{91}\mathrm{Pa}
\xrightarrow[27.4~\mathrm{days}]{\beta^-}{}^{233}_{92}\mathrm{U}.
$$

The times shown are half-lives. A fresh core initially contains only
${}^{232}\mathrm{Th}$ and ${}^{235}\mathrm{U}$, so the initial number densities
of ${}^{233}\mathrm{Pa}$ and ${}^{233}\mathrm{U}$ are zero. Assume a constant
${}^{232}\mathrm{Th}$ number density, constant neutron flux $\phi$, and
constant effective microscopic cross sections. Neutron capture in thorium
therefore occurs at the constant rate $\Sigma_a^{\mathrm{Th}}\phi$ per unit
volume, with thorium absorption treated as radiative capture in this model.

- **(a)** Neglect the short half-life of ${}^{233}\mathrm{Th}$ by treating its
  decay to ${}^{233}\mathrm{Pa}$ as instantaneous. Write and solve the
  differential equation for the ${}^{233}\mathrm{Pa}$ number density as a
  function of time. Include both radioactive decay and neutron absorption
  losses; absorption in ${}^{233}\mathrm{Pa}$ cannot be neglected.
- **(b)** Write and solve the differential equation for the
  ${}^{233}\mathrm{U}$ number density as a function of time. Include production
  from ${}^{233}\mathrm{Pa}$ decay and removal by neutron absorption,
  including fission. Neglect radioactive decay of ${}^{233}\mathrm{U}$ on the
  timescale of interest.

Give symbolic solutions in terms of the specified production rate, flux,
microscopic absorption cross sections, and the ${}^{233}\mathrm{Pa}$ decay
constant. State the initial conditions used in each part.
