## Problem 1 [FNRP 5.1]

Determine the mean neutron lifetime against absorption, $l_\infty$, in each
of the following infinite, homogeneous media. Treat the neutrons as
monoenergetic and use the specified absorption cross sections.

- **(a)** Graphite containing $0.0253~\mathrm{eV}$ neutrons, with
  $\Sigma_a=0.273\times10^{-3}~\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$.
- **(b)** Iron containing $1~\mathrm{MeV}$ neutrons, with
  $\Sigma_a=0.738\times10^{-3}~\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$.

A $0.0253~\mathrm{eV}$ neutron has speed $2200~\mathrm{m/s}$. Use the
nonrelativistic relationship between speed and kinetic energy to determine
the speed needed in part (b). State each lifetime in seconds and in a
convenient smaller unit.

## Problem 2

In class, we defined the mean neutron speed as

$$
\bar v=
\frac{\displaystyle\int_0^\infty\phi(E)\,dE}
     {\displaystyle\int_0^\infty\frac{\phi(E)}{v(E)}\,dE}.
$$

Is this consistent with FNRP Eq. (3.44)? Explain by relating the
energy-dependent neutron flux $\phi(E)$ to the energy-dependent neutron
number density $\widetilde n'''(E)$. Identify the weighting used in the
textbook definition and show the equivalence algebraically.

## Problem 3

The infinite-medium reactivity of a batch of fuel is approximated by the
linear reactivity model

$$
\rho_\infty(B)=\rho_0-AB=0.243-0.00664B,
$$

where reactivity is dimensionless and $B$ is in $\mathrm{MWd/kg}$ of initial
heavy metal. Thus, $A=0.00664~(\mathrm{MWd/kg})^{-1}$.

Consider equilibrium $N$-batch refueling with identical batch masses and
equal reactivity weights. Each batch accumulates the same burnup increment
per cycle; the oldest batch is discharged at the end of each cycle. Assume
a constant leakage penalty $\rho_L=0.05$, subtracted from the
batch-averaged infinite-medium reactivity, and criticality at the end of
each cycle.

- **(a)** Estimate the cycle burnup increment $B_c^{(N)}$ and discharge
  burnup $B_d^{(N)}$ for $N=1,2,3,4,5$. Report the results in a table.
- **(b)** Determine the limiting discharge burnup as $N\to\infty$.

Here, “cycle length” means a burnup increment, not a time interval; no
specific power is supplied to convert burnup to days.
