## Problem 1 [FNRP 5.4]

Suppose that a fissile material is discovered for which all fission neutrons
are emitted promptly. A reactor fueled with this material has a constant
neutron lifetime $l=0.002~\mathrm{s}$, and its neutron population is governed
by FNRP Eq. (5.22). Assume a fixed flux shape, so fission power is
proportional to neutron population. Neglect feedback.

The reactor is initially critical and has no external neutron source. At
$t=0$, impose a step change in reactivity and hold the new value constant.

- **(a)** What reactor period is required for the power to triple in
  $10~\mathrm{s}$?
- **(b)** Determine the required multiplication factor $k$ and reactivity
  $\rho=(k-1)/k$. Express $\rho$ as a dimensionless quantity and in pcm.

The reactor period is the time required for the neutron population to
increase by a factor of $e$. One pcm is $10^{-5}$ in dimensionless
reactivity.

## Problem 2

Consider the infinite, homogeneous water–uranium medium from Homework 13,
Problem 2, but assume that all fission neutrons are prompt. The two-group
cross sections are repeated below; all are in $\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$.

| Quantity | Group 1 | Group 2 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $\Sigma_\gamma$ | $2.04\times10^{-3}$ | $1.91\times10^{-2}$ |
| $\Sigma_f$ | $2.96\times10^{-4}$ | $1.38\times10^{-2}$ |
| $\overline\nu\Sigma_f$ | $7.38\times10^{-4}$ | $3.36\times10^{-2}$ |
| $\Sigma_{s,1\gets g}$ | $7.21\times10^{-1}$ | $0$ |
| $\Sigma_{s,2\gets g}$ | $4.27\times10^{-2}$ | $2.72$ |

Use $\Sigma_{a,g}=\Sigma_{\gamma,g}+\Sigma_{f,g}$ and mean group speeds
$\bar v_1=10^6~\mathrm{m/s}$ and $\bar v_2=2.2\times10^3~\mathrm{m/s}$.
For this one-group transient approximation, assume a fixed spectrum
with

$$
\frac{\phi_1(t)}{\phi_2(t)}
=\frac{\Sigma_{a,2}}{\Sigma_{s,2\gets1}}.
$$

This is the steady two-group spectral shape for fast-only fission and
external sources with no upscatter. Impose this shape throughout the
transient, rather than solving separate time-dependent group equations.

Initially the neutron population is zero. At $t=0$, switch on a uniform
fast-neutron source of strength
$S_0=100~\mathrm{neutrons\,cm^{-3}\,s^{-1}}$ and hold it constant.
Neglect changes in the material properties and feedback.

- **(a)** Determine the effective one-group absorption cross section
  $\bar\Sigma_a$.
- **(b)** Determine the mean neutron speed $\bar v$. Be consistent about
  converting meters to centimeters.
- **(c)** Determine the mean neutron lifetime $l_\infty$.
- **(d)** Collapse $\overline\nu\Sigma_f$ with the same flux weights and
  determine $k_\infty$ and $\rho_\infty$ for the one-group model.
- **(e)** Write and solve the initial-value problem for the total neutron
  number density $n'''(t)$. Compute and plot the **volumetric absorption
  rate**, $R_a(t)=\bar\Sigma_a\bar v\,n'''(t)$, for
  $0\le t\le0.1~\mathrm{s}$. Determine its steady limit and compare it with
  Homework 13, Problem 2. Explain why the steady limits agree even though
  a fixed spectrum is an approximation during the transient.

## Problem 3: Pulsed Accelerator-Driven Reactor

Consider an idealized one-group accelerator-driven subcritical reactor with

$$
k=0.95,\qquad l=10^{-4}~\mathrm{s},\qquad
\bar\nu=2.5,\qquad E_f=200~\mathrm{MeV/fission}.
$$

Here, $E_f$ is recoverable fission energy. A proton accelerator sends
**1-GeV protons** to a neutron-producing target. The beam current is
**10 mA during each 1-ms pulse**, and pulses begin every **10 ms**. The
current is zero between pulses. Each incident proton supplies **20 effective
source neutrons** to the reactor; this yield already includes target-to-core
coupling. The accelerator draws **25 MW of electrical power while the beam
is on** and zero between pulses.

Assume a fixed spatial shape and energy spectrum, constant reactor
properties, all fission neutrons emitted promptly, and negligible feedback
and depletion. Count only fission energy as reactor thermal power; neglect
direct beam heating. The parameters define a hypothetical teaching model.
Use $e=1.602176634\times10^{-19}~\mathrm{C}$ and
$1~\mathrm{eV}=1.602176634\times10^{-19}~\mathrm{J}$.

After many pulses, the neutron population is periodic:
$n(t+T_c)=n(t)$, where $T_c=10~\mathrm{ms}$ includes both the on- and
off-time. For any quantity $X(t)$, define

$$
\langle X\rangle=\frac{1}{T_c}\int_0^{T_c}X(t)\,dt.
$$

- **(a)** Determine the duty factor, proton rate and neutron source
  strength during a pulse, and cycle-averaged neutron source strength.
  Also calculate the proton beam power while on and its cycle average.
  Distinguish beam power from the specified electrical consumption.
- **(b)** Starting from

  $$
  \frac{dn}{dt}=\frac{k-1}{l}n(t)+S(t),
  $$

  integrate over one **complete cycle** to determine the average neutron
  population without solving the transient. Do not assume that $n(t)$ is
  constant during the cycle or zero between pulses.
- **(c)** The fission-neutron production rate is $k n(t)/l$.
  Determine the cycle-averaged fission rate and reactor thermal power.
  Calculate the fission energy released per cycle and compare the average
  thermal power with the average accelerator electrical consumption.
  Explain why this comparison alone does not determine net electrical
  output.
- **(d)** Predict how the average thermal power and electrical consumption
  change if the pulse duration doubles to $2~\mathrm{ms}$ while the
  repetition period and peak current remain fixed. Then compare the
  original pulsed case with a continuous beam having the **same average
  current**. Does pulsing itself increase average reactor thermal power
  under these assumptions?

In this problem, $n(t)$ is the total neutron population and $S(t)$ is the
total source rate in neutrons/s, not a source density.
