## Problem 1  

A power reactor is fueled with slightly enriched uranium. At the
end of core life 30% of the power comes from the fissioning of
the built up plutonium-239. Calculate the effective value of $\beta$ at
the beginning and at the end of core life; determine the percent
increase or decrease.

## Problem 2

Consider a uranium-fueled reactor that is initially critical with a neutron
density of $n'''(0)=10^6~\mathrm{cm^{-3}}$. Solve the kinetics equations
numerically and plot the resulting neutron density for $t\in[0,10]~\mathrm{s}$
for the following cases using the six-group precursor data in Table 5.1:

**(a)** A positive step in reactivity to go up in power characterized by
the following reactivity:

$$
\rho(t)=
\begin{cases}
0 & 0\le t<1,\\
0.25\beta & 1\le t<6,\\
0 & 6\le t\le10.
\end{cases}
$$

**(b)** A negative step in reactivity to go down in power characterized by
the following reactivity:

$$
\rho(t)=
\begin{cases}
0 & 0\le t<1,\\
-0.25\beta & 1\le t<6,\\
0 & 6\le t\le10.
\end{cases}
$$

Here, $t$ is in seconds. Remember, you need to find the appropriate precursor
concentrations for steady state to use for initial conditions.
You may assume that $\Lambda=50\times10^{-6}~\mathrm{s}$ for simplicity.
