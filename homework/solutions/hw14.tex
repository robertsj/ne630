\noindent{\bf Homework 14 solution draft: three-group graphite spectrum and
infinite-medium multiplication.}

\subsection*{Group structure and notation}

Use the three energy groups
\[
\begin{array}{ccl}
g=1 &:& 10^5 < E \le 10^6\ \eV \quad\text{fast},\\
g=2 &:& 1 < E \le 10^5\ \eV \quad\text{intermediate/epithermal},\\
g=3 &:& E \le 1\ \eV \quad\text{thermal}.
\end{array}
\]
The upper bound \(10^6\ \eV\) is the source energy used for the graphite
fixed-source problem and is also the representative starting energy used in
the simple eigenvalue model.  The actual fission spectrum is not
monoenergetic; the approximation made here is to set \(\chi_1=1\) and
\(\chi_2=\chi_3=0\).

Let \(\phi_g=\int_g \phi(E)\dd E\) be the group-integrated flux.  For any
reaction \(x\),
\[
\Sigma_{x,g}=\sum_i N_i\sigma_{x,i,g}10^{-24},
\]
where \(\sigma\) is in barns and \(N_i\) is the atom density of isotope \(i\).
Absorption already includes fission:
\[
\sigma_{a,i,g}=\sigma_{\gamma,i,g}+\sigma_{f,i,g}.
\]

\subsection*{General three-group balances}

The general infinite-medium multigroup balance is
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{r,g}\phi_g
= S_g
+ \sum_{\substack{h=1\\h\ne g}}^3 \Sigma_{s,g\leftarrow h}\phi_h
+ \frac{\chi_g}{k_{\mathrm{eff}}}s_f''' .
\label{eq:general-balance}
\end{equation}
In words: removal from group \(g\) balances the external source, scattering
into group \(g\), and fission neutrons born into group \(g\).  The removal
cross section is
\[
\Sigma_{r,g}=\Sigma_{a,g}
+\sum_{\substack{h=1\\h\ne g}}^3 \Sigma_{s,h\leftarrow g},
\]
because within-group scattering does not change the group population.

With only adjacent downscatter retained, the only nonzero intergroup
scattering terms are \(\Sigma_{s,2\leftarrow1}\) and
\(\Sigma_{s,3\leftarrow2}\); all upscatter and direct \(1\to3\) transfer are
neglected.  The
nonmultiplying fixed-source graphite balances become
\begin{align}
(\Sigma_{a,1}+\Sigma_{s,2\leftarrow1})\phi_1 &= s_0,\\
(\Sigma_{a,2}+\Sigma_{s,3\leftarrow2})\phi_2
  &= \Sigma_{s,2\leftarrow1}\phi_1,\\
\Sigma_{a,3}\phi_3 &= \Sigma_{s,3\leftarrow2}\phi_2.
\label{eq:fixed-source}
\end{align}
The corresponding source-free eigenvalue balances are
\begin{align}
(\Sigma_{a,1}+\Sigma_{s,2\leftarrow1})\phi_1
  &= \frac{s_f'''}{k_{\mathrm{eff}}},\\
(\Sigma_{a,2}+\Sigma_{s,3\leftarrow2})\phi_2
  &= \Sigma_{s,2\leftarrow1}\phi_1,\\
\Sigma_{a,3}\phi_3 &= \Sigma_{s,3\leftarrow2}\phi_2,
\label{eq:eig-balances}
\end{align}
where
\[
s_f'''=\sum_{g=1}^3 \nu\Sigma_{f,g}\phi_g
\]
is the volumetric fission-neutron source rate.  With
\(\chi_1=1\), all fission neutrons are born into group 1 in this model.
For isotope- and group-dependent fission yields, replace
\(\nu\Sigma_{f,g}\) by
\(\sum_i N_i\bar{\nu}_{i,g}\sigma_{f,i,g}10^{-24}\).

\subsection*{Elastic group transfer}

For isotope \(i\), define
\[
\alpha_i=\left(\frac{A_i-1}{A_i+1}\right)^2.
\]
With isotropic scattering in the center-of-mass frame, the outgoing energy
from an incident energy \(E\) is uniformly distributed over
\(\alpha_iE\le E'\le E\):
\[
p_i(E\to E')=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-\alpha_i)E},&
\alpha_iE\le E'\le E,\\[0.7em]
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Equivalently, the probability that a collision from energy \(E\) lands in
an energy interval \([E_L,E_U]\) is
\[
P_i(E\to [E_L,E_U])
=\frac{\left[\min(E,E_U)-\max(\alpha_iE,E_L)\right]_+}
{(1-\alpha_i)E},
\]
where \([z]_+=\max(z,0)\).

For the adjacent downscatter closure used here, assume a \(1/E\) flux in a
group and a group-constant scattering cross section.  The elastic kernel then
gives the usual average lethargy gain per collision,
\[
\xi_i=1+\frac{\alpha_i}{1-\alpha_i}\ln\alpha_i.
\]
The effective microscopic transfer cross section across the lower boundary of
group \(g\) is
\[
\sigma_{s,g+1\leftarrow g}^{(i)}
\simeq \sigma_{s,i,g}\frac{\xi_i}{\Delta u_g},
\qquad
\Delta u_g=\ln\left(\frac{E_{g-1}}{E_g}\right).
\]
Here \(\Delta u_1=\ln(10)\) and \(\Delta u_2=\ln(10^5)\).

\subsection*{Problem 1: pure graphite fixed-source spectrum}

The carbon atom density is
\[
N_C=\frac{\rho_C}{M_C}\NA
=\frac{2.26}{12.0}(6.02214076\times10^{23})
=1.13416984\times10^{23}\ \mathrm{atoms\,cm^{-3}}.
\]
Thus one barn corresponds to \(0.113416984\ \cm^{-1}\) for this graphite.

The macroscopic cross sections are
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
\Sigma_{a,1}=1.39503\times10^{-4},&
\Sigma_{a,2}=1.50724\times10^{-5},&
\Sigma_{a,3}=3.40251\times10^{-4},\\
\Sigma_{s,2\leftarrow1}=1.83399\times10^{-2},&
\Sigma_{s,3\leftarrow2}=7.47582\times10^{-3}.&
\end{array}
\]
All values are in \(\cm^{-1}\).

Solving Eq.~\eqref{eq:fixed-source} in sequence gives
\[
\phi_1=\frac{s_0}{\Sigma_{a,1}+\Sigma_{s,2\leftarrow1}}
=54.1144\,s_0,
\]
\[
\phi_2=\frac{\Sigma_{s,2\leftarrow1}\phi_1}
{\Sigma_{a,2}+\Sigma_{s,3\leftarrow2}}
=132.488\,s_0,
\]
and
\[
\phi_3=\frac{\Sigma_{s,3\leftarrow2}\phi_2}{\Sigma_{a,3}}
=2910.95\,s_0.
\]
Since \(s_0\) has units \(\mathrm{n\,cm^{-3}\,s^{-1}}\), these coefficients
have units of cm and the fluxes have units
\(\mathrm{n\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}\).

As a balance check,
\[
\Sigma_{a,1}\phi_1+\Sigma_{a,2}\phi_2+\Sigma_{a,3}\phi_3=s_0,
\]
to rounding.  In this model nearly all source neutrons are eventually absorbed
after reaching the thermal group.

\subsection*{Problem 2: \(k_{\mathrm{eff}}\) as a function of \(R=N_C/N_U\)}

For 2 atom-percent enrichment,
\[
N_U=\frac{N_C}{R},\qquad
N_{235}=0.02\,\frac{N_C}{R},\qquad
N_{238}=0.98\,\frac{N_C}{R}.
\]
Let \(c=N_C10^{-24}=0.113416984\).  Define uranium-mixture microscopic
coefficients
\[
u_{x,g}=0.02\sigma_{x,235,g}+0.98\sigma_{x,238,g}.
\]
From the table above,
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrr}
g & u_{f,g} & u_{a,g} & u_{\gamma,g}\\
\hline
1 & 0.32232 & 0.37958 & 0.05726\\
2 & 0.47268264 & 24.36565761 & 23.89297497\\
3 & 10.10001029 & 14.1916 & 4.09158971
\end{array}
\]
in barns.  The uranium-mixture transfer coefficients are
\[
u_{t,12}=0.02693037,\qquad u_{t,23}=0.00690485
\]
in barns.

Therefore
\[
\Sigma_{a,g}(R)=
c\left(\sigma_{a,C,g}+\frac{u_{a,g}}{R}\right),
\qquad
\Sigma_{f,g}(R)=c\frac{u_{f,g}}{R},
\]
and
\[
\Sigma_{s,2\leftarrow1}(R)
=c\left(0.161702956+\frac{0.02693037}{R}\right),
\qquad
\Sigma_{s,3\leftarrow2}(R)
=c\left(0.065914510+\frac{0.00690485}{R}\right).
\]

Normalize the eigenvector by \(\phi_1\), and define
\[
x(R)=\frac{\phi_2}{\phi_1},\qquad y(R)=\frac{\phi_3}{\phi_1}.
\]
The lower two rows of Eq.~\eqref{eq:eig-balances} give
\[
x(R)=
\frac{\Sigma_{s,2\leftarrow1}(R)}
{\Sigma_{a,2}(R)+\Sigma_{s,3\leftarrow2}(R)},
\qquad
y(R)=\frac{\Sigma_{s,3\leftarrow2}(R)x(R)}{\Sigma_{a,3}(R)}.
\]
The first row then gives
\[
k_{\mathrm{eff}}(R)=
\frac{\sum_{g=1}^3 \nu\Sigma_{f,g}(R)\phi_g}
{(\Sigma_{a,1}(R)+\Sigma_{s,2\leftarrow1}(R))\phi_1}.
\]
Equivalently, because the downscatter terms cancel in a global balance,
\[
k_{\mathrm{eff}}(R)=
\frac{\nu\Sigma_{f,1}(R)+\nu\Sigma_{f,2}(R)x(R)+\nu\Sigma_{f,3}(R)y(R)}
{\Sigma_{a,1}(R)+\Sigma_{a,2}(R)x(R)+\Sigma_{a,3}(R)y(R)}.
\]
The one-group fixed-source view can still reserve \(k_\infty\) as a material
property and use \(k_{\mathrm{eff}}\) as the eigenvalue knob, so production
appears through the replacement \(k_\infty\to k_\infty/k_{\mathrm{eff}}\).

\textbf{Gap to note.}  FNRP Table 3.2 does not provide fission neutron yields
\(\bar{\nu}_{i,g}\).  A numerical value of \(k_{\mathrm{eff}}\) therefore needs either
a separate data source or an explicit named approximation for \(\nu\).  If a
common value \(\nu=2.43\) is used for all fissions, then at \(R=1000\),
\[
x=1.78865,\qquad y=6.85861,
\]
and
\[
\boxed{k_{\mathrm{eff}}(R=1000;\ \nu=2.43)=1.04793.}
\]
This number should be read as the value of the stated three-group closure, not
as a continuous-energy benchmark.

\subsection*{Assumptions and remaining gaps}

\begin{itemize}
\item Fast-group cross sections are fission-spectrum averages from Table 3.2,
not pointwise 1 MeV cross sections.
\item Intermediate fission and absorption data are resonance integrals divided
by \(\ln(10^5)\), corresponding to a \(1/E\) flux from 1 eV to 0.1 MeV.
\item Intermediate scattering cross sections are not supplied by Table 3.2;
the thermal scattering averages are used for group 2.
\item Transfer is adjacent downscatter only.  Upscatter and direct
\(1\to3\) transfer are omitted.
\item Resonance self-shielding is neglected, so the resonance-integral
absorption is an infinite-dilution approximation.
\item A numerical multiplication factor requires fission neutron yields.
Those are not in Table 3.2 and must be supplied as a named prerequisite or
explicit approximation.
\end{itemize}
