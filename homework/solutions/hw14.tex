\noindent{\bf Problem 1}.

Consider a very large graphite block with density $2.26\,\mathrm{g\,cm^{-3}}$
and a uniformly distributed source of 1 MeV neutrons with strength
$s_0\,\mathrm{n\,cm^{-3}\,s^{-1}}$.

Using the data supplied in Table 3.2 of FNRP, construct the necessary cross
sections and transfer probabilities for a three-group approximation of the
neutron energy spectrum. State all assumptions.

\begin{adaptedsolution}
We use the course three-group structure, ordered from high to low energy,
\begin{align*}
  (E_0,E_1,E_2,E_3)&=(10^7,10^5,1,10^{-3})\ \mathrm{eV},\\
  g=1 &: 0.1\ \mathrm{MeV}<E<10\ \mathrm{MeV},\\
  g=2 &: 1\ \mathrm{eV}<E<0.1\ \mathrm{MeV},\\
  g=3 &: 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{eV}<E<1\ \mathrm{eV}.
\end{align*}
The 1 MeV source is therefore entirely in group 1. The calculation makes the
following assumptions.
\begin{itemize}
  \item The medium is infinite, homogeneous, and in steady state; leakage and
        spatial dependence are neglected.
  \item Scattering is elastic from stationary nuclei and isotropic in the
        center-of-mass system. Upscattering is neglected.
  \item Within each nonthermal group, the collision density is represented by
        a $1/E$ spectrum. A single carbon collision cannot skip a group for
        these boundaries, so only $1\to2$ and $2\to3$ transfer is retained.
  \item Table 3.2 fast constants are used in group 1 and thermal constants in
        group 3. In group 2,
        $\sigma_{a,2}=0.0869 I_a$; because Table 3.2 does not give an
        intermediate-energy scattering integral, the tabulated thermal
        scattering cross section is used in groups 2 and 3.
  \item Neutrons scattered below $10^{-3}$ eV are ignored. This is negligible
        for the present three-group balance but makes the finite group bounds
        explicit.
\end{itemize}

For elastic scattering from a nucleus of mass number $A$,
\begin{equation*}
  \alpha_A=\left(\frac{A-1}{A+1}\right)^2,
  \qquad
  \xi_A=1+\frac{\alpha_A\ln\alpha_A}{1-\alpha_A}.
\end{equation*}
For a $1/E$ spectrum and a constant scattering cross section, the fraction of
collisions that cross the lower boundary of group $g$ is
\begin{equation*}
  P_{g+1\leftarrow g}^{(A)}
  =\frac{\xi_A}{\ln(E_{g-1}/E_g)}.
\end{equation*}
For carbon ($A=12$),
\begin{equation*}
  \alpha_C=0.715976,\qquad \xi_C=0.157769,
\end{equation*}
and hence
\begin{equation*}
  P_{2\leftarrow1}^{(C)}=0.0342591,
  \qquad
  P_{3\leftarrow2}^{(C)}=0.0137036.
\end{equation*}
The resulting carbon microscopic constants are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c c c c c c}
\toprule
$g$ & $\sigma_{a,g}$ & $\sigma_{s,g}$ & $P_{g+1\leftarrow g}$
    & $\sigma_{s,g+1\leftarrow g}$ & $\sigma_{s,g\leftarrow g}$ \\
& \multicolumn{5}{c}{barns, except for $P$} \\
\midrule
1 & $1.2300\times10^{-3}$ & 2.36 & 0.0342591 & 0.0808515 & 2.27915 \\
2 & $1.32957\times10^{-4}$ & 4.81 & 0.0137036 & 0.0659145 & 4.74409 \\
3 & $3.0000\times10^{-3}$ & 4.81 & --- & 0 & 4.81 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Here $\sigma_{s,g+1\leftarrow g}=P_{g+1\leftarrow g}\sigma_{s,g}$ and
$\sigma_{s,g\leftarrow g}=\sigma_{s,g}-\sigma_{s,g+1\leftarrow g}$.

Using $M_C=12.011\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$ gives
\begin{equation*}
  N_C=\frac{\rho N_A}{M_C}=1.13313\times10^{23}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}.
\end{equation*}
The macroscopic absorption, downscatter, and removal constants are then
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c c c c}
\toprule
$g$ & $\Sigma_{a,g}$ & $\Sigma_{s,g+1\leftarrow g}$ &
$\Sigma_{r,g}=\Sigma_{a,g}+\Sigma_{s,g+1\leftarrow g}$ \\
& \multicolumn{3}{c}{$\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$} \\
\midrule
1 & $1.39375\times10^{-4}$ & $9.16153\times10^{-3}$ & $9.30091\times10^{-3}$ \\
2 & $1.50658\times10^{-5}$ & $7.46898\times10^{-3}$ & $7.48404\times10^{-3}$ \\
3 & $3.39939\times10^{-4}$ & 0 & $3.39939\times10^{-4}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

With no upscatter, the infinite-medium fixed-source equations are triangular:
\begin{align*}
  \Sigma_{r,1}\phi_1 &= s_0,\\
  \Sigma_{r,2}\phi_2 &= \Sigma_{s,2\leftarrow1}\phi_1,\\
  \Sigma_{a,3}\phi_3 &= \Sigma_{s,3\leftarrow2}\phi_2.
\end{align*}
Thus the approximate spectrum is
\begin{equation*}
  \boxed{
  (\phi_1,\phi_2,\phi_3)
  =(107.516,\ 131.615,\ 2891.79)\,s_0
  \ \mathrm{cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}.}
\end{equation*}
As a useful check,
$\Sigma_{a,1}\phi_1+\Sigma_{a,2}\phi_2+\Sigma_{a,3}\phi_3=s_0$
to rounding: in an infinite nonmultiplying medium, every source neutron is
eventually absorbed.
\end{adaptedsolution}

\noindent{\bf Problem 2}.

Now consider the case in which uranium enriched to 2\% U-235 (by atom) is
uniformly dispersed in the graphite block. Determine the resulting
infinite-medium multiplication factor $k_\infty$ as a function of the C-to-U
atomic ratio $R=N_C/N_U$. State all assumptions.

\begin{adaptedsolution}
We retain the group structure and scattering model from Problem 1. In
addition, we assume
\begin{itemize}
  \item a homogeneous mixture with
        $N_{235}=0.02N_U$, $N_{238}=0.98N_U$, and $R=N_C/N_U$;
  \item all fission neutrons are born in group 1, so
        $\boldsymbol{\chi}=(1,0,0)^T$;
  \item $\nu=(2.49,2.43,2.43)$ for the fast, intermediate, and thermal
        groups, respectively;
  \item resonance self-shielding, heterogeneous effects, inelastic
        scattering, and $(n,2n)$ reactions are neglected.
\end{itemize}
The values of $\nu$ are not included in FNRP Table 3.2 and are therefore an
explicit auxiliary approximation. As in Problem 1, group-2 absorption and
fission cross sections are obtained from $0.0869 I_a$ and $0.0869 I_f$.

The uranium-mixture microscopic constants are atom-fraction-weighted values,
for example
\begin{equation*}
  \sigma_{x,U}=0.02\sigma_{x,235}+0.98\sigma_{x,238}.
\end{equation*}
For scattering transfer, the same weighting is applied after evaluating
$\xi_A$ for $A=235$ and 238. The resulting values are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c c c c c}
\toprule
$g$ & $\sigma_{f,U,g}$ & $\sigma_{a,U,g}$
    & $\sigma_{s,U,g+1\leftarrow g}$ & $(\nu\sigma_f)_{U,g}$ \\
& \multicolumn{4}{c}{barns} \\
\midrule
1 & 0.322320 & 0.379580 & 0.0134652 & 0.802577 \\
2 & 0.472906 & 24.377188 & 0.00690485 & 1.14916 \\
3 & 10.100010 & 14.191600 & 0 & 24.5430 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Because every macroscopic cross section contains the common factor
$N_U10^{-24}$, that factor cancels from the eigenvalue. Define microscopic
mixture coefficients, in barns,
\begin{align*}
  a_1(R)&=0.001230R+0.379580,&
  d_1(R)&=0.080851478R+0.013465186,\\
  a_2(R)&=0.000132957R+24.377188,&
  d_2(R)&=0.065914510R+0.006904846,\\
  a_3(R)&=0.003000R+14.191600.&&
\end{align*}
Here $a_g$ is absorption and $d_g$ is transfer from group $g$ to $g+1$.
The removal coefficients are
\begin{equation*}
  r_1=a_1+d_1=0.082081478R+0.393045186,
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
  r_2=a_2+d_2=0.066047467R+24.384092846,
  \qquad r_3=a_3.
\end{equation*}

Normalize the fundamental-mode flux to $\phi_1=1$. The group-2 and group-3
balances give
\begin{equation*}
  y(R)\equiv\frac{\phi_2}{\phi_1}=\frac{d_1}{r_2},
  \qquad
  z(R)\equiv\frac{\phi_3}{\phi_1}=\frac{d_1d_2}{r_2r_3}.
\end{equation*}
The group-1 balance then gives the requested multiplication factor:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
  k_\infty(R)=
  \frac{0.8025768+1.1491624\,y(R)+24.5430250\,z(R)}
       {0.082081478R+0.393045186}.}
  \label{eq:hw14-kinf}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, substituting the definitions of $y$ and $z$ into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:hw14-kinf} gives a single explicit rational function of $R$.

Some representative values are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c c@{\qquad}c c}
\toprule
$R$ & $k_\infty$ & $R$ & $k_\infty$ \\
\midrule
0    & 2.044 & 500  & 0.915 \\
10   & 0.723 & 1000 & 1.043 \\
100  & 0.468 & 2000 & 1.019 \\
300  & 0.763 & 5000 & 0.774 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Within this deliberately coarse three-group model, $k_\infty=1$ at
approximately
\begin{equation*}
  R=5.38,\qquad 726.35,\qquad 2232.87.
\end{equation*}
The nonmonotonic behavior is physical in outline: a nearly pure enriched-
uranium mixture is fast critical; initial carbon addition removes fast
neutrons before providing enough thermalization; further moderation raises
$k_\infty$; and very large $R$ eventually dilutes the fuel and increases
parasitic absorption in carbon. The numerical crossings should not be treated
as design values because resonance self-shielding and heterogeneous-lattice
effects, both important in real graphite--uranium systems, were excluded.
\end{adaptedsolution}
